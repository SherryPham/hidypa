What is the future of AI?

This is a tricky one, because there's always the question "What is AI?" There is, by its very nature, a huge amount of uncertainty about future applications of AI – whether it'll work on all of the problems at hand – and even more questions about the impact it could have on the entire environment.

However, there is now, for many non-invasive robotic technologies, a much more complex and potentially useful answer. If it were to work effectively in the wild, it would, for example, work in 3D where "all the elements" are fairly simple, which makes it possible to study nature at an even larger scale than we currently can.

The important things that're important for making a sense of it, of course, are how it behaves, how it behaves within some sense, how it works, even how it works in our everyday lives. But this can be difficult for AI systems to understand, so it's critical that we go a step further, and understand what's happening around us: how our will or that of the AI system relates to our current environments or actions, and what kinds of things are happening. We'll also want to understand whether those things relate to what's happening at the moment our systems are interacting with each other, within their own contexts. But we're also very very limited, so what's really important is how our will or that of the AI system relates to that of the environment.
